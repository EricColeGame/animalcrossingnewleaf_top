If your 3DS still gets regular playtime, animal crossing new leaf amiibo villagers are the biggest reason to keep a stack of figures and cards next to your console. The free "Welcome amiibo" update turned a 2012-era town builder into a crossover celebration, adding Zelda heroes, Splatoon idols, and Sanrio friends to your campground. ## Why Animal Crossing New Leaf Amiibo Villagers Still Matter

For most of the game's life, the easiest way to get a specific villager was to trade with other players online or hope the random campsite delivered someone good. That changed twice: once when the Welcome amiibo update arrived, and again when Nintendo shut down 3DS online services in April 2024. Trading over the internet is gone. Amiibo scanning is not — it's a purely local feature that works offline, forever.

That makes the figures and cards the only reliable way to choose your neighbors. You don't need luck, you don't need a friend with a Dream Address, and you don't need to reset your town hoping for a better roster.

| Method | Works today? | How much control you get |
|---|---|---|
| Amiibo figures and cards | Yes | Pick the exact villager |
| Random campsite visitors | Yes | None — whoever shows up |
| Local wireless trading | Yes | High, but needs a nearby player |
| Online trading and visits | No (ended April 2024) | Not available anymore |

For anyone returning to the game after a long break, that table is the whole story: the social shortcuts are gone, and the plastic shortcuts are the ones that survived.

## How the Welcome amiibo Update Works

The amiibo functionality wasn't part of New Leaf at launch. It arrived in a free update released on November 2, 2016, which also added the campground, RV interiors, and a new currency called MEOW coupons. If your copy of the game has never been updated, none of the scanning features will respond — you'll tap a figure against the reader and nothing will happen.

Before you scan anything, confirm your hardware can read amiibo at all. Newer systems have the reader built in; older ones need an accessory that Nintendo sold separately.

| Console | Built-in NFC? | What you need to scan |
|---|---|---|
| New Nintendo 3DS / New 3DS XL | Yes | Nothing extra |
| New Nintendo 2DS XL | Yes | Nothing extra |
| Nintendo 3DS / 3DS XL | No | Nintendo 3DS NFC Reader/Writer |
| Nintendo 2DS | No | Nintendo 3DS NFC Reader/Writer |

Once your hardware is ready, the routine is short and always the same:

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Download the Welcome amiibo update. The 3DS eShop no longer sells new games, but software updates remain available to download. |
| 2 | Confirm your NFC setup — built-in or reader accessory. |
| 3 | Travel to the campground north of your town. |
| 4 | Interact with the amiibo reader at the campground and tap your figure or card. |
| 5 | Step inside the RV, talk to the camper, and ask them to move in. |

The camper only accepts if your town has room — with ten villagers already living there, you'll need to let someone move out first. You can read more about official figure compatibility on [Nintendo's amiibo hub](https://www.nintendo.com/us/amiibo/).

## Every Amiibo-Exclusive Villager You Can Invite

Eight villagers in New Leaf exist only through amiibo. Four come from The Legend of Zelda, three are Splatoon-inspired, and one is a Monster Hunter crossover. None of them appear through normal campsite visits, so scanning is the only way to get them.

| Villager | Theme | Amiibo that summons them |
|---|---|---|
| Epona | Zelda | Link (Majora's Mask, Ocarina of Time, Rider), Sheik |
| Ganon | Zelda | Bokoblin, Ganondorf, Guardian, 8-bit Link |
| Medli | Zelda | Toon Link and most other Zelda figures |
| Wolf Link | Zelda | Most Link figures, Zelda (Super Smash Bros.) |
| Cece | Splatoon (styled after Callie) | Callie |
| Viché | Splatoon (styled after Marie) | Marie |
| Inkwell | Splatoon | Any Inkling figure from the Splatoon or Smash Bros. lines |
| Felyne | Monster Hunter | Navirou, Barioth and Ayuria |

Notice how the Zelda figures split across four different campers. Link's amiibo from Majora's Mask, Ocarina of Time, and the Rider series all bring Epona, while a generic Link figure usually brings Wolf Link instead. If you're chasing one specific camper, check the chart before you buy — the difference between an Epona and a Wolf Link is often just which Link you tap.

Every one of these campers arrives with a themed RV stocked with furniture you can't get anywhere else. That furniture is bought with MEOW coupons rather than Bells, so it's worth hoarding coupons before you invite someone over.

## Which Cards and Figures Work

Figures get all the attention, but the Animal Crossing amiibo card line does most of the heavy lifting. The cards fall into distinct groups, and each group behaves differently at the campground.

| amiibo type | RV visit? | Can move in? | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome amiibo series cards (including the Sanrio collaboration) | Yes | Yes | RV visit, themed furniture, full move-in |
| Non-SP cards from Series 1–4 and Promos | No | Yes | Wisp offers an item or arranges a move-in |
| SP cards (Blanca, Blathers, and similar) | No | No | A single special item |
| Blathers and Celeste figures | Yes | No | Campsite visit or a gift item |
| Villager (Super Smash Bros. series) | No | No | Summons Wisp |

The pattern is simple: if a card belongs to the Welcome amiibo series, expect an RV and a new neighbor. If it's an older card, expect Wisp instead — he's the spirit who handles scanning for the earlier sets and offers you a choice between a gift and a new resident.

### Special Characters Who Visit but Never Move In

Some of the best-loved characters in the game will happily show up and then refuse to unpack. Blathers and Celeste can be summoned with their Animal Crossing figures, and both will visit the campground or hand over an item, but neither can become a villager. The same goes for Wisp, who appears when you tap the Smash Bros. Villager figure, and for SP cards such as Blanca, which only produce a special item. If you're collecting for move-ins, these are nice bonuses rather than roster additions.

## Tips for Getting the Amiibo Villagers You Want

Scanning is forgiving, but a few habits save real time and money.

| Tip | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| Keep at least one empty plot | Amiibo campers can't move in when your town already has ten residents. |
| Save before you scan | If you tap the wrong figure, you can quit without saving and try again. |
| Match the figure to the villager | Zelda amiibo map to four different campers, so verify before buying. |
| Buy single cards instead of packs | Cheaper when you only want one specific neighbor. |
| Pick up an NFC reader early | Older 3DS and 2DS models are useless for scanning without one. |
| Bank MEOW coupons | RV furniture is coupon-only, and you'll want several pieces. |

One more thing worth knowing: community reports suggest that amiibo campers behave exactly like ordinary villagers after they move in. They'll send letters, request favors, and eventually ask to move away like anyone else. Scanning a figure gets them in the door — keeping them is up to you.

## FAQ

**Do I need the Welcome amiibo update to use animal crossing new leaf amiibo villagers?**

Yes. Scanning does nothing in an unpatched copy of the game. The update is free and still downloadable, and it's what adds the campground, the RV interiors, and MEOW coupons.

**Can I scan amiibo with an original 3DS or 2DS?**

You can, but not directly. Those models have no built-in NFC reader, so you need the Nintendo 3DS NFC Reader/Writer accessory. New Nintendo 3DS, New 3DS XL, and New 2DS XL systems scan figures and cards out of the box.

**Which amiibo unlocks the most villagers?**

The Splatoon line is the most efficient — any Inkling figure brings Inkwell, while the Callie and Marie figures each unlock Cece and Viché. Zelda figures are the opposite: they split four different campers between them, so a single Link doesn't cover the whole set.

**Can amiibo villagers move out later?**

They can. Once they've settled in, amiibo campers follow the same routines as any other resident, including eventually asking to leave. If you want them to stay permanently, decline the request — or scan their amiibo again later to bring them back.

**Are the Sanrio cards real amiibo?**

Yes. The Sanrio collaboration cards were released as part of the Welcome amiibo card series, so they scan like any other card from that set: RV visit, themed furniture, and a villager who can move into your town.
