Animal Crossing New Leaf items are the engine that keeps the game running long after your final home loan is paid off. Every fishing trip, villager request, and Re-Tail run feeds one giant collection: your catalog. Once you understand how those Animal Crossing New Leaf items are grouped, where they come from, and which ones can never be reordered, you stop wasting Bells and start collecting with a plan.

This guide walks through the major item categories, the sources that stock them, the orderable-versus-unorderable split, and a practical tracking system for finishing your catalog without burning out.

## What Counts as an Item in Animal Crossing: New Leaf?

Almost anything you can hold, wear, place, or donate counts as an item. That covers the obvious — furniture, wallpaper, flooring, shirts, hats, and shoes — and the easy-to-forget entries like stationery, K.K. Slider songs, gyroids, fossils, and fruit.

Your catalog, viewable through the Nookling store, registers an entry the moment you pick something up. You don't have to buy it or keep it. Picking an item up and dropping it again is enough to log it, which is why players routinely catalog expensive sets in a friend's town and hand them straight back.

Fan-built search tools and community databases such as [Nookipedia's item database](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Item) let you filter by orderable status, amiibo+ content, and customization options — genuinely useful when you're hunting one missing piece of a set.

That list alone explains why collectors describe New Leaf as one of the deepest item pools in the series. Each group behaves differently, and each one has its own acquisition rules.

## Furniture, Fashion, and Everyday Gear

### Furniture and Home Décor

Furniture is the largest slice of the catalog and the one most players obsess over. Matching series — Alpine, Cabin, Classic, Exotic, Green, Lovely, Minimalist, Modern, Ranch, Regal, Robo, Sleek, Stripe, and the luxury Gracie sets — each run eight to thirteen pieces, and most have a matching wall and floor.

Cyrus at Re-Tail is the customization specialist. Bring him certain furniture pieces plus ore (ruby, sapphire, emerald, amethyst, silver, gold) or a custom pattern, and he will refurbish the item into a variant that registers as a separate catalog entry. Player experience suggests this is the single most overlooked way to inflate your catalog count, because a customized piece counts as a distinct entry from its original.

### Clothing and Fashion

Clothing rotates daily. Able Sisters handles tops, bottoms, dresses, hats, and accessories, while Kicks stocks shoes and socks. Labelle's accessory line sits inside the Able Sisters shop and rotates on its own schedule.

GracieGrace, which opens on the upper floor of T&T Emporium, sells seasonal luxury clothing and furniture in limited runs. Community reports consistently flag Gracie stock as one of the most time-sensitive parts of the catalog: if you miss a season, you wait a full year or trade with another player.

### Tools and Everyday Gear

Tools are cheap, orderable, and easy to replace — until you need a golden one.

Golden versions exist for the shovel, axe, net, rod, watering can, and slingshot, and they're earned through long-term milestones rather than bought. They are unorderable, so losing one hurts.

## Orderable vs. Unorderable Items

This is the distinction that matters most for collectors. Orderable items can be re-bought from the catalog at any time. Unorderable items can only be obtained again through their original source — or by trading.

When you browse the catalog, unorderable entries simply won't offer a purchase option. That's your signal to hang onto the item rather than sell it.

| Item type | Orderable? | Examples | How to get another |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard furniture | Yes | Most themed series | Catalog order |
| Standard clothing | Yes | Able Sisters stock | Catalog order |
| Basic tools | Yes | Shovel, net, rod | Catalog or shop |
| Event items | No | Holiday furniture | Wait for the event, or trade |
| Region-locked items | No | Region-exclusive gifts | Trade with players abroad |
| Gracie items | No | Gracie furniture series | GracieGrace, seasonal |
| Island items | No | Mermaid set, toy hammer | Tortimer Island medals |
| amiibo / RV items | No | Campground furniture | amiibo plus MEOW Coupons |
| Villager photos | No | Villager pics | Friendship milestones |
| Fish, bugs, fossils, art | No | Museum donations | Catch or dig them |
| Gyroids | No | Buried gyroids | Dig after rain or snow |

A simple rule: if you can buy it again tomorrow, sell duplicates freely. If you can't, store it.

## Smart Tips for Cataloging Animal Crossing New Leaf Items

Cataloging is a marathon, so treat it like one. A few habits make the difference between steady progress and a storage locker full of junk.

- **Sweep the shops daily.** Two minutes a day beats a five-hour catch-up session.
- **Buy the cheap stuff first.** Low-cost furniture and clothing fill catalog slots fast and free up Bells for the rare pieces.
- **Catalog, then sell.** Pick up an item to register it, then sell it at Re-Tail if you don't need it. Re-Tail pays more than Nook's.
- **Never sell unorderables.** Event, Gracie, Island, and RV items are one-shot acquisitions unless you trade.
- **Use Cyrus.** Customized variants are separate catalog entries, and ore is easy to collect.
- **Host cataloging parties.** In community reports, trading sessions where players pick up and drop whole sets are the fastest way to fill gaps.
- **Track what's missing.** A simple spreadsheet by category stops you from re-buying things you already own.
- **Bank MEOW Coupons.** They're the only currency for campground RV items.

Rarity tiers are worth internalizing, because they tell you where to spend your time.

| Tier | Examples | Why they're hard to get |
|---|---|---|
| Event-locked | Holiday and festival furniture | One narrow window per year |
| Region-locked | Region-exclusive distributed items | Trade is the only route |
| amiibo-locked | Campground RV furniture | Requires a figure or card plus Coupons |
| Island-locked | Mermaid set, toy hammer | Medals and repeated island trips |
| Random | Saharah walls and floors, Redd stock, balloon drops | Pure luck per visit |

If you're starting fresh, prioritize the daily shop sweep and the random sources. Events and amiibo items will still be there when you're ready for them.

## FAQ About Animal Crossing New Leaf Items

**Can you reorder every item from the catalog?**
No. Orderable items — most furniture, clothing, and basic tools — can be re-bought anytime. Event, Gracie, Island, amiibo, and villager photo items are unorderable, and the catalog won't offer a purchase option for them. If you sell an unorderable Animal Crossing New Leaf item, your only way back is trading with another player.

**What are the rarest Animal Crossing New Leaf items?**
Rarity usually comes from access rather than price. Event furniture, region-exclusive distributed items, campground RV furniture, and Island prizes top most community lists. Gracie's seasonal luxury sets also qualify, since missing a season means waiting a full year.

**How do I tell whether an item is orderable?**
Check it in the catalog at the Nookling store. If a price and purchase option appear, it's orderable. If the entry shows no purchase option, treat it as permanent — store it instead of selling it. Fan search tools also let you filter by orderable status, which is faster when you're planning a set.

**Do I need amiibo to complete a collection?**
Not to enjoy the game, but a meaningful slice of Animal Crossing New Leaf items — the campground RV furniture added in the Welcome amiibo update — requires an amiibo figure or card plus MEOW Coupons. If you're chasing a full catalog, plan for that category early rather than discovering the gap at the end.

Cataloging in New Leaf rewards patience more than speed. Sweep the shops, learn which sources carry which categories, protect your unorderables, and let the collection grow one daily visit at a time.
