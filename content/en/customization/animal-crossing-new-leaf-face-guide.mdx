Your face in Animal Crossing: New Leaf is permanent, and you only get one chance to choose it during the opening train ride with Rover. That's why an Animal Crossing New Leaf face guide is worth opening before you even name your town — three quick answers decide your eyes and expression for the entire life of your save file. This Animal Crossing New Leaf face guide explains how those questions work, how to read the charts, and what to do if you end up with a face you don't like.

## Why Your Character's Face Is Locked In on the Train

Animal Crossing: New Leaf doesn't hand you a character creator with sliders and presets. Instead, it hides the whole process inside a conversation. When you start a new save file, Rover sits across from you on the train and asks about your move to a new town. Your replies determine your face and eye shape.

Everything else about your character is negotiable. Clothes come from shops, hairstyles come from Shampoodle, and even your skin tone shifts after long summer days on the island. Your face does not. That design choice is the entire reason players treat the train ride like an exam rather than a cutscene.

The cost of getting it wrong is real. If you dislike your face, your only in-game solution is deleting the town and starting fresh — which wipes your town name, your carefully picked map, your house placement, and every item you've collected. Restarting on day one costs you minutes. Restarting in month three costs you everything.

## How Rover's Questions Decide Your Face

Rover asks three questions during the train ride, and your answers form a short sequence. A well-made Animal Crossing New Leaf face guide lists those answers in the exact order he asks them and pairs each complete sequence with a picture of the face it produces.

The first question is the one about how often you visit the town you're moving to. Its choices include "I've never been there," "I'm moving," and "I'll get a place there." The questions that follow touch on your reasons for moving and how you feel about arriving. The final answer is often the one that tips the result toward a specific face, which means changing a single word can land you on a completely different character.

Here's how two sample sequences from a typical chart are laid out:

| Sequence | 1st answer | 2nd answer | 3rd answer | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | I've never been there. | I'm moving. | I'll get a place there. | One face (chart images 1–2) |
| B | I've never been there. | I'm moving. | I'm sure I'll be fine. | A different face (chart images 3–4) |

Two sequences that look nearly identical produce different results — which is exactly why writing your answers down beats trying to remember them mid-conversation. Charts usually show two images per sequence because the face you get differs depending on whether you picked a boy or a girl at the start.

One more thing worth knowing: the answers you give also shape the small talk Rover makes on the ride. He responds to what you tell him, so if you're following a chart, expect the dialogue around your answers to vary while the choices themselves stay the same.

## Reading a Face Chart Without Guesswork

The most common reason players end up with the wrong face isn't a bad chart — it's mismatched wording. Plenty of guides are translated from another language or written from memory, so the text on the chart doesn't match what appears on your screen. That's the appeal of the JVGS Animal Crossing New Leaf face guide: it uses the exact wording from the English release, so there's no guessing at synonyms or half-remembered phrasing.

If you're playing in a non-English language, expect the choices to read differently. Either track down a chart written for your language or match the on-screen options by position rather than by literal meaning.

## What to Look For in a Face

Once you've narrowed down the options in the Animal Crossing New Leaf face guide, spend a minute thinking about how you'll actually see that face. It shows up in every dialogue box, on your Town Pass Card, in photos at the photo booth, and in the player list when friends visit your town. A face that looks cute in a thumbnail can feel very different after a hundred hours.

| Feature | What to consider | Player experience |
|---|---|---|
| Eye shape | Wide, round eyes read as cheerful; narrow or half-closed eyes read as calm or sleepy | The single most noticeable difference between faces |
| Eye color | Interacts with your hair color and skin tone | Some colors stand out more against certain hair shades |
| Mouth and expression | Shifts the overall mood of the face more than you'd expect | Hard to judge from small chart images |
| Overall vibe | Pick something you'd still like after a long break from the game | Most players keep one face for the life of the town |

Community reports suggest that the safest approach is to look at several chart images, including close-ups if the guide offers them, rather than committing based on one small picture. If you're torn between two options, ask which one you'd be happy to see in every screenshot for the next year.

## FAQ

**Can you change your face in Animal Crossing: New Leaf after you start?**
No. Once the train conversation with Rover ends, your face and eye shape are locked to that save file. Hair and clothing can be changed later, but the only route to a new face is deleting the town and starting over. That's why an Animal Crossing New Leaf face guide is something you use before you begin, not after.

**How many questions does Rover ask?**
Three. They're asked in sequence during the train ride, and the combination of your answers determines the face you receive. The first question covers how often you visit the town you're moving to.

**Does eye color change with the face you pick?**
Yes. Eye color is tied to the face you receive, so it's set during the same opening conversation. There's no in-game way to adjust it afterward.

**Is the face guide for Animal Crossing: New Leaf the same for other Animal Crossing games?**
No. Every game in the series handles appearance differently. The GameCube title and City Folk have their own charts, and New Horizons replaced the system entirely with a mirror you can use whenever you like.
