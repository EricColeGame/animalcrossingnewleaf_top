Standing on the beach at 4 a.m. with a fishing rod in hand is one of the most profitable habits in Animal Crossing: New Leaf — and one of the easiest to get wrong. Cast into the wrong stretch of water at the wrong hour and you will pull up the same three cheap fish all season long. ## How Fishing Works in Animal Crossing: New Leaf

Every entry in an Animal Crossing New Leaf fish guide ultimately comes down to one piece of gear. You cannot catch a single fish without a fishing rod — nets do not work on water — and Timmy and Tommy sell the basic rod at the Nookling Junction from the very start of the game. Buying one should be among your first purchases after the initial tent loan.

Once the rod is equipped, face any body of water and press A to cast. The line lands where you aim it, and after a few seconds a fish shadow will drift toward your bobber. The moment the float is pulled completely under, press A again to reel in. React too early and the fish escapes; too late and it swims off with your bait-free hook.

Community reports consistently point to the same trick for timing: listen for the soft "plop" sound instead of watching the bobber. The audio cue fires slightly before the visual animation resolves, which makes it far more reliable during laggy sessions or when a big shadow is drifting in.

Rod upgrades exist for dedicated anglers. A Silver Rod becomes available through the Museum Shop once you have donated enough specimens, and the Golden Rod is a long-term reward for serious collectors. Neither changes how fish behave — they simply give you more attempts before a rod needs replacing, and more room for error.

### Shadow Sizes at a Glance

Community guides usually number fish shadows from 1 (smallest) to 6 (largest). The number itself does not appear in-game, but the visual size does, and it is the fastest way to know whether a shadow is worth casting at.

| Shadow size | What it looks like | What usually hides underneath | Casting tip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Tiny ripple, barely visible | Goldfish, tadpoles, small river fish | Easy to miss — scan slowly near the surface |
| 2 | Small, compact oval | Anchovy, angelfish, guppy | Great early-game Bells; common in rivers and along the shore |
| 3 | Medium oval | Everyday river and pond fish | Expect repeats; useful for filling the encyclopedia |
| 4 | Noticeably large | Bigger freshwater fish and some sea species | Bites feel slower — stay patient |
| 5 | Very large, slow drift | Eels and heavy ocean fish | Often appears near the dock or river mouth |
| 6 | Huge, unmistakable | Sharks and the largest ocean species | Rare; usually requires sea or island waters |

## Fish Availability: Seasons, Times, and Locations

This is the backbone of any Animal Crossing New Leaf fish guide. Every species in the game has four fixed conditions: a month window, a time window, a water type, and a shadow size. Miss any one of them and the fish simply will not appear, no matter how long you stand there.

The table below covers a representative sample. The Anchovy and Angelfish figures come straight from community-compiled data, while the remaining rows describe well-known patterns that most long-term players confirm.

| Fish | Where | Season | Time window | Shadow | Value |

| Anchovy | Sea | All year | 4 AM – 9 PM | Small (size 2) | 200 Bells |
| Goldfish | River | All year | All day | Tiny | Modest |

A few patterns jump out immediately. The midday window is the thinnest for fishing, while the stretch between 4 PM and 9 AM carries the widest variety. Weather also matters more than most players realize — the Coelacanth only appears during rain or snowfall, which means a rainy evening is genuinely the best possible time to grab your rod.

### Tortimer Island: Permanent Summer Fishing

Tortimer Island is the single most valuable location covered by any Animal Crossing New Leaf fish guide, because the island stays locked in summer regardless of what the calendar says on your 3DS. Summer-only ocean species — including the big-money sharks — can be caught there in January just as easily as in July.

Getting there requires unlocking the island through the standard story progress and paying the boat fare to Kapp'n, but the return on that investment is enormous. Bring an empty inventory, fish the island's surrounding waters at night, and you can fill your pockets with high-value catches before the boat returns. Just remember that island catches still count toward your encyclopedia, so do not sell every single one.

## Rare and High-Value Fish Worth Chasing

New Leaf contains 72 fish in total, and 12 of them were brand new to the series when the game launched. Among the newcomers are the soft-shelled turtle, tadpole, mitten crab, nibble fish, saddled bichir, blowfish, and ribbon eel. That mix of species is the part of an Animal Crossing New Leaf fish guide that most players care about once the common catches are out of the way.

The practical strategy is simple: chase the weather-dependent fish first. Rainy and snowy days are rare compared to any given summer evening, so when precipitation starts, drop whatever you are doing and head to the beach. Everything else on that list can wait for a normal session.

## Tips for Completing the Museum and Fish Encyclopedia

A complete Animal Crossing New Leaf fish guide strategy is less about reflexes and more about scheduling. You cannot catch everything in one weekend, so treat the encyclopedia as a month-by-month project.

- **Fish the edges of each month.** Species appear and disappear on the first of the month, so the last few days of one month and the first few of the next are your best chances to catch both outgoing and incoming fish.
- **Donate before you sell.** Blathers only needs one of each species, but it must be the first one you hand over. Sell duplicates at Re-Tail afterward.
- **Play the windows, not the hours.** A 30-minute session at 5 AM is worth more than three hours at noon.
- **Keep a written checklist.** With 72 species and overlapping conditions, memory alone will fail you.
- **Bank your island trips.** Island fishing funds everything else — rods, house upgrades, and Public Works Projects.
- **Do not ignore weather.** Rain and snow unlock a small set of otherwise impossible catches.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many fish are there in Animal Crossing: New Leaf?**
There are 72 fish in total, and 12 of them were introduced in New Leaf, including the soft-shelled turtle, tadpole, mitten crab, nibble fish, saddled bichir, blowfish, and ribbon eel.

**Do I need a fishing rod to catch fish?**
Yes. Fishing rods are sold at the Nookling Junction early in the game, and no other tool will let you pull a fish out of the water. Buy one as soon as you can afford it.

**Can I catch summer fish in winter?**
Yes — on Tortimer Island. The island remains in a permanent summer state all year round, so summer-only species are catchable there in any month, which is why this Animal Crossing New Leaf fish guide recommends it so heavily.

**What is the fastest way to make Bells with fishing?**
Fish the island at night for large shadows, prioritize rainy days for weather-locked species like the Coelacanth, and sell everything you do not donate at Re-Tail rather than the Nookling store.
