## What Are Animal Crossing New Leaf Ordinances?

Becoming mayor is the biggest change New Leaf made to the Animal Crossing formula, and ordinances are the clearest example of that power. Animal Crossing New Leaf ordinances are town-wide policies you pass at the Town Hall that permanently bend the rules of your town — when shops open, how fast weeds spread, and how much Bells you pocket when you sell a shark.

You can only have one active at a time, and every ordinance costs 20,000 Bells to enact. That's a serious chunk of change in the early game, which is exactly why it pays to understand all four options before you walk up to the counter.

Get the choice right and the game quietly adapts to your real-life schedule. Get it wrong and you'll spend weeks fighting your own town. Here's everything you need to know.

| Ordinance | Core Effect | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Beautiful Town | Flowers never wilt, fewer weeds, no trash in water, no cockroaches | Busy players and gardeners |
| Early Bird | Shops open and close 3 hours earlier; villagers wake earlier | Morning players |
| Night Owl | Shops open and close 3 hours later; villagers stay up later | Evening and night players |
| Bell Boom | Shop buy and sell prices both rise by roughly 20% | Players grinding Bells |

## All Four Animal Crossing New Leaf Ordinances Explained

Each policy solves a different problem. Below is what each one actually does in practice, based on in-game behavior and long-running community reports.

### Beautiful Town Ordinance

This is the maintenance-free option. Flowers stop wilting entirely, so you never have to water them again — a huge quality-of-life win if you're breeding hybrids or decorating with flower beds. Weed spawn rates drop noticeably, garbage stops appearing in your river and ocean, and cockroaches no longer show up in your house.

One persistent myth is worth clearing up: the Beautiful Town Ordinance does **not** stop villagers from moving out. It only affects the environment. If you want a tidy town without daily chores, this is your pick.

### Early Bird Ordinance

Early Bird shifts the entire town three hours earlier. Shops open three hours sooner and close three hours sooner, and villagers are up and wandering around much earlier in the morning.

If you play before work or school, this turns dead time into productive time — you can hit the shops, check turnip prices, and catch morning-only bugs and fish before your day starts. The trade-off is brutal for night owls: closing time arrives three hours sooner than you're used to.

### Night Owl Ordinance

The mirror image of Early Bird. Shops open three hours later and stay open three hours later, and villagers remain active well into the evening. Community reports consistently name this the most popular choice among players who only get to play after dinner.

If you've ever loaded up your town at 10 p.m. and found every door locked, Night Owl is the fix. Just remember that morning play sessions become nearly useless for shopping.

### Bell Boom Ordinance

Bell Boom raises prices in both directions: you pay roughly 20% more in shops, and you receive roughly 20% more when you sell. It doesn't create free money — it amplifies whatever you're already doing.

Sell more than you buy and it's a straight upgrade. Buy more than you sell and it quietly drains your wallet. This is the ordinance for players aggressively paying off home loans or saving for a big public works project.

| Ordinance | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Beautiful Town | Zero watering chores, fewer weeds, cleaner water, no roaches | No effect on shop hours or Bell prices |
| Early Bird | Shops and villagers active 3 hours earlier | Shops also close 3 hours earlier |
| Night Owl | Shops and villagers active 3 hours later | Shops also open 3 hours later |
| Bell Boom | ~20% more Bells from everything you sell | ~20% more Bells to buy anything in a shop |

## How to Enact an Ordinance at Town Hall

Changing your town's policy takes less than a minute, but the steps trip up new mayors because only one player can do it.

| Step | What To Do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Play as the mayor — the first player character created in the town |
| 2 | Enter the Town Hall during business hours |
| 3 | Speak to Isabelle at the front counter |
| 4 | Choose the ordinance option from her menu |
| 5 | Select the ordinance you want and confirm |
| 6 | Pay the 20,000 Bell fee |
| 7 | Wait until the next in-game day for it to take effect |

A few important rules apply. You cannot stack ordinances — enacting a new one replaces the old one immediately. Additional human players living in your town can't change policy; only the mayor holds that authority. If you're unsure whether your town already has one active, Isabelle will tell you when you ask.

## Costs, Timing, and Other Rules

Ordinances are a recurring investment, not a one-time purchase. Every time you switch policies, you pay the fee again. Many mayors treat this as a seasonal decision: Night Owl during a busy work period, Beautiful Town when life gets chaotic.

| Detail | Rule |
|---|---|
| Cost to enact | 20,000 Bells |
| Cost to change | 20,000 Bells each time |
| Number active at once | One |
| Takes effect | The next in-game day |
| Who can enact | The mayor only |
| Where | Town Hall front counter |
| Reversible? | Yes — simply enact a different ordinance |

One more consideration: some services run on their own schedules and don't fully follow the shop-hour shift. Re-Tail in particular keeps its long daily hours regardless of which ordinance you choose, according to player experience. The museum, the island, and other special locations also operate independently of your town policy.

## Which Ordinance Should You Choose?

Match the ordinance to how you actually play, not how you wish you played. The table below is the fastest way to decide.

| If You... | Choose |
|---|---|
| Play before work or school | Early Bird |
| Play late in the evening | Night Owl |
| Play at random times and forget to water flowers | Beautiful Town |
| Are grinding Bells for a home loan or bridge | Bell Boom |
| Are breeding hybrid flowers | Beautiful Town |
| Are working toward a perfect town rating | Beautiful Town |
| Rarely buy furniture or clothing from shops | Bell Boom |

A practical strategy many mayors use is to start with **Bell Boom** while their loan is still large and their town is underdeveloped. Once the loan is paid off and the town is decorated, switch to **Beautiful Town** to eliminate daily maintenance. Players with tight schedules often skip both and simply pick whichever shop-hour ordinance matches their free time.

### Quick Tips to Avoid Regret

- **Don't enact Bell Boom before a big shopping spree.** A full furniture set or a stack of saplings costs noticeably more.
- **Beautiful Town is the strongest "set and forget" pick.** It removes chores rather than adding benefits.
- **Switch freely.** Because the fee is a flat 20,000 Bells, changing your mind is always affordable later in the game.
- **Check shop hours after switching.** The three-hour shift catches people off guard for the first few days.
- **Early Bird and Night Owl don't stack.** You can't have both, so pick the half of the day that matters more.
- **The island runs on its own clock.** Ordinance changes don't affect Tortimer Island's schedule.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I have more than one Animal Crossing New Leaf ordinance active at the same time?**

No. Only one ordinance can be active in your town at any given moment. Enacting a new one automatically replaces the previous policy, and you'll pay the 20,000 Bell fee again for the change.

**How much does it cost to change ordinances in New Leaf?**

Every enactment or change costs 20,000 Bells, paid to Isabelle at the Town Hall counter. There's no discount for switching back to an ordinance you've used before.

**Does the Beautiful Town Ordinance stop villagers from moving away?**

No — this is a common misconception. Beautiful Town prevents flowers from wilting, reduces weeds, keeps trash out of the water, and stops cockroaches from appearing. Villager move-outs are governed by separate friendship and timing mechanics.

**How long does it take for an ordinance to take effect?**

The new policy begins on the next in-game day. If you enact an ordinance today, shop hours and other effects will reflect the change tomorrow.

**Do ordinances affect the island or the museum?**

Generally, no. Tortimer Island and several special facilities keep their own schedules. The three-hour shop shift from Early Bird and Night Owl applies mainly to the shops in your main street area, according to community reports.
